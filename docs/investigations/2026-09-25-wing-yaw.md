## Conventions

The derivatives below are in classical **FRD body axes** (x forward, y right, z down), converted from SimLab body
axes (x back, y right, z up) with L = −Mx, N = −Mz, p = −ωx, r = −ωz. β > 0 means the air comes from the right. With
these signs **Cnβ > 0 is weathercock-stable**, Clβ < 0 is a stable dihedral effect and Cnr < 0 is yaw damping.
Rates are made non-dimensional with b/2V. Reference values: S = 0.2475 m², b = 1.10 m, V = 13 m/s, sea level,
power at trim. Trim at 13 m/s is α 6.07°, elevator −0.044 and throttle 0.595 (T = 1.08 N).
δa is per unit aileron command (±1 = ±20° elevon).

Sideslip responses:

- **(a) 0.15 aileron for 0.5 s:** peak β is 5.4 to 6.1° (adverse, nose away from the turn), then it rings −4.1, +2.6,
  −2.3, +1.1°.
- **Larger input, 0.3 aileron for 1 s:** peak β is **13.4°**, with later swings of about 8°. This matches the flight log's
  ±13°.
- **Speed dependence (0.3 aileron for 1 s):** 15.3° at 12 m/s (α 7.5°) and 9.0° at 16 m/s (α 3.3°). β scales with the
  trim α.
- **(c) Turbulence:** the app's defaults are wind 0 and turbulence 0, so the defaults give no excitation at all. With
  3 m/s and 6 m/s wind at turbulence 1 and a wings-level aileron pilot, β has an sd of 0.28° / 0.51° (max 0.9° /
  1.5°). Turbulence is not what makes it feel loose. The Dryden model has no rotational or gradient gust terms
  (p_g, r_g), so it barely excites yaw.

The wing has about 4–5× less weathercock stiffness than the tailed models and 11–15× less yaw damping.

External references:

- **Conventional aircraft:** Cnβ is typically about 0.05–0.2 /rad (Etkin & Reid; Nelson, *Flight Stability and
  Automatic Control*).
- **Vertical tail volume:** here Vv = Sv·lv/(S·b) = 0.024·0.175/0.272 = **0.015**. Raymer's historical values are
  0.02 for sailplanes and about 0.04 for homebuilts and GA singles (*Aircraft Design*, Table 6.4). The winglets are
  below the lowest class.
- **MIL-F-8785C Dutch roll minimums, Level 1:** ζ ≥ 0.08 and ζωn ≥ 0.15 for Cat B/C; ζ ≥ 0.19 and ζωn ≥ 0.35 for
  Cat A. The sim's model-scale ζωn is 0.43. Froude-scaled to a ~10 m span (time × 3) it is ≈ 0.14. That is
  borderline Level 1/2 for Cat B and fails the Level 1 ζ for Cat A. ζ itself does not depend on scale.
- **MIL-F-8785C spiral:** Level 1 requires a time to double of at least 12–20 s. The sim's 7.6 s is about 23 s at full
  scale, so it is acceptable.
- **Beard & McLain Zagi model** (*Small Unmanned Aircraft*, App. E), from memory, please verify: Cnβ ≈ 0 and
  Cnr ≈ −0.004. Tailless RC wings are known for weak directional stiffness and damping, so the sim's values are
  plausible for the type.

## 2. Contributions and winglet modelling

- **Winglets provide essentially all of Cnβ (0.039) and 80% of Cnr (−0.013).** The side force at β = +1° is −0.056 N
  per winglet. Both winglets have the same sign and point in the right direction, so the mirroring is correct: there
  is no sign bug. The quarter-chord segments sit 0.157 and 0.192 m behind the CG (mean arm 0.175 m ≈ 0.16 b). The
  root at x 0.45 is consistent with the wing tip (tip LE 0.432, TE 0.545). The strip AR is 2.4, from the mirrored
  pair. This happens to equal the end-plated single-panel value 2·b²/S, which is reasonable for a winglet end-plated
  by the wing.
- **Wing sweep: Cnβ ≈ 0 in the strip model.** The decomposition gives −0.0018 from the lift part, because the
  windward panel's extra lift tilts forward by (α − αi) in body axes, and +0.0020 from the drag part. The DATCOM/Seckel
  wing formula, CL²[1/(4πA) − tanΛ/(πA(A+4cosΛ))·(cosΛ − A/2 − A²/(8cosΛ) + 6x̄ sinΛ/A)] with CL 0.42, A 4.89, Λ 25°
  and x̄ 0.08, gives about +0.006. The strip model misses this because its induced drag uses a global 1/(πeAR) with
  no spanwise redistribution in sideslip. The gap is about 15% of the total Cnβ.
- **Wing Cnr = −0.003 (strip).** A DATCOM-style estimate is about −0.007 (Cnr/CL²·CL² + Cnr/CD0·CD0; the chart values
  are from memory). So the strip model is probably about 2× low here.
- **Pod:** the linear cdA crossflow at 4 cm ahead of the CG gives −0.002. A Munk-moment estimate for a ~0.5 L pod would
  be about −0.004, so the model is slightly optimistic.
- **Prop:** there is no propeller normal (side) force in the model. The P-factor offset only moves the thrust point
  vertically in sideslip, which gives pitch, not yaw. A pusher disk 0.23 m behind the CG would add roughly Cnβ +0.004,
  which is stabilizing, so the model is conservative.
- **Not modelled:** tip-vortex sidewash and the end-plate interaction at the winglet root, and winglet toe or cant.

## 3. Model bug or airframe characteristic?

**No sign or mirroring bug was found.** Every missing term is small. Their effect in the linear model (base: ζ 0.101,
T2 7.6 s):

| Change | ζ | Time to double |
|---|---|---|
| Wing Cnβ +0.006 | 0.096 | |
| Wing Cnr −0.008 | 0.113 | |
| Both | 0.108 | |
| Prop fin (Cnβ +0.004, Cnr −0.002) | 0.101 | |
| Pod Munk (Cnβ −0.002) | 0.103 | |
| Cnr ×2 | 0.125 | 10.9 s |
| Cnp = 0 | 0.126 | |
| Clβ ×0.5 | 0.116 | |

**Root cause: a genuine airframe characteristic, made larger by estimated (unmeasured) winglets.**

1. **The winglets are small and have a short arm** (Vv 0.015, lv/b 0.16). This gives low Cnβ, and very low Cnr
   because Cnr/Cnβ ≈ 2·lv/b. The result is a lightly damped (ζ ≈ 0.1), mostly-yaw Dutch roll: the wag.
2. **The wing trims at α ≈ 6° body.** Rolling about the body x axis converts α into β (β̇ ≈ p·sinα, the body-axis
   Cnp of −0.065). With weak Cnβ the nose does not follow, so every roll input produces 5–13° of adverse sideslip that
   then rings. This is why β grows at low speed (15° at 12 m/s) and shrinks at high speed (9° at 16 m/s).
3. **Increasing the winglets raises Cnβ, Cnr and CYβ together.** Because of this ζ only grows as about √size (0.10 →
   0.12). The main gains are a smaller forced sideslip and faster settling, not a large rise in ζ.

## 4. Ranked fixes

**1. Data: larger winglets, moved aft (0.12 → 0.14 span, root x 0.45 → 0.48).** The winglets then overhang the TE by
about 2.5 cm. Only do this if the real airframe measures like this: the winglets are "estimated". Measured with the
spike and the behavior tests (hull `finTop*` moved to [0.51, ±0.55, 0.14]):

| Metric | Shipped | Candidate |
|---|---|---|
| Cnβ | 0.038 | 0.058 |
| Cnr | −0.016 | −0.026 |
| DR period | 1.49 s | 1.25 s |
| DR ζ | 0.10 | 0.11 |
| Time from 5° slip until \|Δβ\| < 2.5° | 1.53 s | 0.77 s |
| 0.15 aileron pulse peak β (Report_wing_handling) | 6.1° | 4.2° |
| 0.3 aileron for 1 s: peak β | 13.4° | 9.4° |
| 0.3 aileron for 1 s: later swing | 7.9° | 3.8° |
| Stall pull | 44°/s | 33°/s |
| Spiral, static margin, trim, hand launch | | unchanged (T2 7.4 s, 7.8% MAC, 22.9 m) |

- **Test impact:** all Flight tests pass except the `wing_launch` golden, which must be regenerated.
- **Larger variants cut β further** (0.16/0.15/0.10 gives 3.3° and 7.5°) **but fail
  `Wing_slow_pull_to_stall_does_not_roll_off`**:

  | Variant (span / root chord / tip chord / root x) | Stall-pull roll rate | Result |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0.14 / 0.14 / 0.09 | 67°/s | fails |
  | 0.15 span (root x 0.45 or 0.48) | 79–93°/s | fails |
  | 0.16 span | 155–225°/s | fails |

  These are deep-stall departures at α 31–37° and elevator ≥ 0.85, where that test is already a knife edge
  (backlog #3).
- **Moving aft alone (3–5 cm) helps little:** 5.4 → 4.9° and ζ 0.105.

**2. Data: smaller elevon aileron throw / expo** (the ±20° elevon throw is large; backlog #3 notes real setups run
about ±10–12° with expo). β scales with roll rate, so halving the aileron authority roughly halves the sideslip per
stick input. It does not change ζ or the period. It also affects `RollRateTests`, `ControlResponseTests` and the launch
pilot, which would need checking.

**3. Model: add the missing small yaw terms.** These are the wing's CL² sweep Cnβ (+0.006) and fuller swept-wing Cnr
(about −0.004 to −0.008), the pusher-prop normal force (+0.004 Cnβ) and the pod Munk moment (−0.002). Adding rotational
turbulence (p_g, r_g) would also improve the turbulence feel. The predicted net effect on the Dutch roll is small:
ζ ≈ 0.10 → 0.11, and the pulse sideslip is about 10% lower. Worth doing for fidelity, preferably checked with VSPAERO
(the planned path), but it will not remove the wag.

Not a fix, but relevant: most real FPV wings fly with a stabilizing flight controller. An optional yaw/roll "gyro"
assist in the game would match what pilots actually experience.

## 5. What I am unsure about

- **Real winglet size and position.** They are estimates, and they dominate the result. Measure the airframe first.
- **Winglet effectiveness at the tip** (sidewash, wing end-plate effect, tip-vortex interaction). It could be ±30%
  either way. The strip model has no sidewash.
- **DATCOM and Zagi reference numbers** are from memory and chart reading. The formula-based wing Cnβ (+0.006) is the
  most reliable of them.
- **Inertia** (0.05 / 0.08 / 0.03): it is consistent with the Zagi values scaled by m·b², but not measured. Izz lower
  by 45% shortens the period to 1.14 s at the same ζ.
- **The flight log's ±13°** came from an earlier version (static margin 3.4%, higher α) and an unknown stick input.
  The 0.3 aileron for 1 s reproduction is suggestive, not a replay.
- **The stall-pull test's sensitivity to winglet size** is probably deep-stall model noise (strip post-stall polars),
  not a real effect. I did not investigate it further.
- **Hands-off heading wander** (spiral T2 7.6 s plus motor torque, −44° bank in 12 s) may also be part of what the user
  calls "unstable in yaw". It is backlog #1/#10 and is not changed by the winglets.
